# Portfolio case study

## Problem

Long-running AI agents can produce large amounts of apparently useful work while failing to reduce the distance to the user's accepted outcome. Common examples include optional refactoring, endless research, stale completion claims, and post-completion polishing.

## Approach

This project developed two portable skills:

- **Astrolabe:** obligation- and evidence-based navigation;
- **Project Atlas:** coordinate-based navigation with route geometry.

The skills were tested through controlled multi-agent experiments with frozen inputs, matched starting states, objective movement scoring, blind final packages, and explicit invalid-result categories.

## What makes the work distinctive

- The artifacts are plain text and portable.
- Progress is tied to observable acceptance, not activity.
- Negative results are published.
- Evaluator defects are treated as research findings.
- The project distinguishes route choice, evidence freshness, alignment, and landing behavior.

## Outcome

The research did not find a universal winner. It found that strong Planner baselines already cover much of goal navigation, while evidence-backed and coordinate models can add auditability and reduce cross-track work in some states. This is a more credible portfolio result than an unsupported claim of superiority.
